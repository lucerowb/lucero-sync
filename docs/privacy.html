Privacy policy

In one sentence: Lucero Sync never sends your photos or personal data to us, and your photos only move between your own devices. The one exception is limited diagnostic data that Google's QR-scanning library sends to Google on Android, explained below.

Lucero Sync is open-source software published by its contributors ("we", "us"). You can check every claim on this page in the source code.

What we collect

Nothing. We don't run any servers, and the app has no analytics, crash reporting, advertising SDKs or remote configuration of our own. We don't receive your photos, metadata, device identifiers, IP address or usage information, and we have no way to.

Data stored on your devices

To work, the app stores the following locally on your phone, in its private storage. It never leaves the device except as described under Network activity.

  • A random device ID, a display name (such as "iPhone-3fa2") and a 6-digit PIN, generated on first launch. They identify the device to phones you pair with.
  • Paired receivers: their names, local IP addresses, ports and PINs.
  • Sync history (sender): for each photo already backed up, its library identifier, filename, size, creation date and a SHA-256 fingerprint of its contents, so it's never sent twice.
  • Received-file index (receiver): the same kind of information for each file received, plus where it was saved.
  • The received photos and videos, in the folder shown in the app.

Uninstalling the app deletes its private data. On Android, received photos in Pictures/LuceroSync stay until you delete them.

iPhone as receiver

On iPhone, received files are stored inside the app (Files › On My iPhone › Lucero Sync). iOS deletes them if you uninstall the app. Move them somewhere else first.

Network activity

The app only talks to devices on your local network:

  • Discovery: the receiver announces itself with Bonjour/mDNS, which includes its display name, device ID and port. Other devices on the same network can see this announcement. The PIN isn't included.
  • Transfer: the sender sends photos, filenames, sizes, creation dates and content fingerprints directly to the paired receiver over HTTP, authenticated with the receiver's PIN.
Unencrypted on your LAN

Transfers aren't encrypted yet. Someone with access to your Wi-Fi who is actively capturing traffic could see photos in transit. Use the app on networks you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi. See the security policy.

On iPhone, if a photo's full-quality original is stored only in iCloud, iOS itself downloads it from Apple when the app requests it. This is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; the app doesn't talk to iCloud directly.

Third parties

The app is built from open-source libraries for photo access, discovery, QR scanning and storage. None of them receive your photos. QR scanning works differently on each platform:

  • iPhone: QR codes are read with Apple's built-in Vision framework, entirely on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Android: QR codes are read with Google's ML Kit barcode-scanning library. The camera image is analysed on the device and isn't sent to Google. However, ML Kit sends Google diagnostic and usage data: device model and OS version, the app's package name and version, performance metrics, scanner settings, error codes and a per-installation identifier that Google says isn't intended to identify you or your device. Google sends this over HTTPS and says it doesn't share it with third parties. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure. ML Kit may send this data whenever it's initialised or used, which can happen even if you never scan a code. We plan to replace ML Kit on Android with an offline scanner that sends nothing.

We don't sell, share or transfer any data, because we don't receive any.

This website

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages and loads fonts from Google Fonts. Those providers may log technical data such as your IP address when you visit, under the GitHub privacy statement and the Google privacy policy. The site itself uses no cookies or analytics.

Children

We don't collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. The app has no accounts and no content directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data handling ever changes, we'll update this page and the "Last updated" date before releasing that change. The full history is in the repository.

Contact

Questions about privacy? Open an issue on GitHub, or report security concerns privately.
