Privacy policy

In one sentence: Lucero Sync never sends your files or personal data to us; they only move, encrypted, between your own devices. The one exception is limited diagnostic data that Google's QR-scanning library sends to Google on Android, explained below.

What we collect

Nothing. We don't run any servers, and the app has no analytics, crash reporting, advertising SDKs or remote configuration of our own. We don't receive your photos, metadata, device identifiers, IP address or usage information, and we have no way to.

Data stored on your devices

To work, the app stores the following locally on each device, in its private app-data folder. It never leaves the device except as described under Network activity.

  • Device identity: a random device ID, a display name and a cryptographic key pair with its certificate. All are generated on first launch, and the private key never leaves the device. The default name comes from the operating system (for example your computer's name or phone model), and you can change it in Settings.
  • Paired devices: their names, platforms, certificate fingerprints, last local IP address and port, the access token this device uses with them, and a hash of the token they use with this device.
  • Backup history (sending device): for each photo already backed up, its library identifier, filename, size, creation date and a SHA-256 fingerprint of its contents, so it's never sent twice.
  • Received-backup index (receiving device): the same kind of information for each backup file received, plus where it was saved.
  • Activity history: file names, sizes, the other device's name, and the full text of text messages you sent or received. You can clear it under Activity.
  • Settings, such as the folders you chose and per-device auto-accept.
  • The files and photos you receive, in the folders shown in Settings › Storage.

Uninstalling the app deletes its private data. On Android and desktop computers, received files and backups in your Pictures and Downloads folders stay until you delete them.

iPhone as receiver

On iPhone, received files are stored inside the app (Files › On My iPhone › Lucero Sync). iOS deletes them if you uninstall the app. Move them somewhere else first.

Network activity

The app only talks to devices on your local network:

  • Discovery: while receiving is on, the device announces itself with Bonjour/mDNS. The announcement includes its display name, device ID, platform (such as "android"), certificate fingerprint and port. Anyone on the same network can see this announcement. It contains no keys, tokens or files.
  • Pairing: the two devices exchange names, platforms, fingerprints, ports and random access tokens over an encrypted connection.
  • Transfers: files, file names, sizes, creation dates, content fingerprints and text go directly to the paired device over TLS 1.3. Each device accepts only the exact certificate it paired with, so others on the network can't read or alter them. Both devices show the progress of a transfer; the sending device tells the receiving one how far a backup has got (counts and the current file name).
  • Web access (off by default): when you turn it on, browsers on your network can open the device's address, sign in with the code shown on the device, send it files and text, and download files you chose to offer. The browser also sees the device's name and what's currently transferring. Sessions end when you turn web access off or close the app.
  • Browser extension: the extension talks only to the Lucero Sync app on the same computer, through an address that isn't reachable from the network. It sends the links, text, images and files you choose, and reads your paired devices' names. It has no analytics. When you send an image, the extension downloads it from the page it's on, as your browser would.
What others on your network can still see

Your device names from discovery, and that two devices are talking to each other and roughly how much data they exchange. They can't see the contents. Use a name that doesn't reveal more than you'd like on shared networks.

On iPhone, if a photo's full-quality original is stored only in iCloud, iOS itself downloads it from Apple when the app requests it. This is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; the app doesn't talk to iCloud directly.

Third parties

The app is built from open-source libraries for photo access, discovery, QR scanning and storage. None of them receive your photos. QR scanning works differently on each platform:

  • iPhone, iPad and Mac: QR codes are read with Apple's built-in Vision framework, entirely on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Windows and Linux: no third-party service is contacted.
  • Android: QR codes are read with Google's ML Kit barcode-scanning library. The camera image is analysed on the device and isn't sent to Google. However, ML Kit sends Google diagnostic and usage data: device model and OS version, the app's package name and version, performance metrics, scanner settings, error codes and a per-installation identifier that Google says isn't intended to identify you or your device. Google sends this over HTTPS and says it doesn't share it with third parties. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure. ML Kit may send this data whenever it's initialised or used, which can happen even if you never scan a code. We plan to replace ML Kit on Android with an offline scanner that sends nothing.

We don't sell, share or transfer any data, because we don't receive any.

This website

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages and loads fonts from Google Fonts. Those providers may log technical data such as your IP address when you visit, under the GitHub privacy statement and the Google privacy policy. The site itself uses no cookies or analytics.

Children

We don't collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. The app has no accounts and no content directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data handling ever changes, we'll update this page and the "Last updated" date before releasing that change. The full history is in the repository.
