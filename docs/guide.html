Set up Lucero Sync

Install the app on both phones, pair them once, and back up your whole Camera Roll. Allow about 15 minutes, plus however long your first sync takes.

Before you start

You'll need:

  • An iPhone running iOS 15 or later. This is usually the sender.
  • An Android phone running Android 7 or later, with enough free space for your library. This is usually the receiver.
  • A Wi-Fi network that both phones can join. Home Wi-Fi is ideal; guest networks often block devices from seeing each other.
  • A Mac with Xcode, but only if you're building the iPhone app yourself (option A below).
Step 1AndroidDownload one APK file and open it. Step 2iPhoneBuild with a free Apple ID, or sideload.

Install on Android

  1. On the Android phone, open the latest release and download lucero-sync.apk.
  2. Open the downloaded file. If Android asks, allow Install unknown apps for your browser or file manager, then tap Install.
  3. Open Lucero Sync and go to the Receive tab.
  4. Tap Grant on the "Save to Pictures/LuceroSync" card and switch on Allow access to manage all files. This lets backups appear in your Gallery and Google Photos. Without it, files are saved in the app's private folder instead.
  5. Go to Settings → Apps → Lucero Sync → Battery and choose Unrestricted, so Android doesn't pause the receiver during long syncs.
Why not the Play Store?

Google Play restricts the "all files access" permission. Installing the APK directly is safe as long as you download it from the official GitHub release.

Install on iPhone

Apple only allows apps from the App Store or apps you sign yourself. Pick one of these options.

Option A: Build it with your free Apple ID (recommended)

  1. On your Mac, install Xcode, Flutter and CocoaPods (brew install cocoapods).
  2. Download the source code: 
    git clone https://github.com/lucerowb/lucero-sync.git
cd lucero-sync
flutter pub get
  3. Create your signing file: 
    cp ios/Flutter/Signing.example.xcconfig ios/Flutter/Signing.local.xcconfig
    Open it and set DEVELOPMENT_TEAM to your Team ID and APP_BUNDLE_ID to something unique, such as com.yourname.lucerosync. To find your Team ID, sign in under Xcode → Settings → Accounts, select your "Personal Team" and look at the Team ID shown there.
  4. On the iPhone, switch on Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode and restart when asked.
  5. Connect the iPhone by USB, unlock it, tap Trust This Computer, and run: 
    flutter run --release
  6. On the first launch iOS says "Untrusted Developer". Go to Settings → General → VPN & Device Management, tap your Apple ID, then Trust.
7-day limit

Apps signed with a free Apple ID stop opening after 7 days. Reconnect the phone and run flutter run --release again; your sync history is kept. A paid Apple Developer account extends this to a year.

Option B: Sideload the prebuilt app

  1. Install AltStore or SideStore on your iPhone by following their instructions.
  2. Download lucero-sync-unsigned.ipa from the latest release on your iPhone.
  3. Open it with AltStore or SideStore, which signs it with your Apple ID and installs it. They can also refresh it before the 7 days run out.

Pair your phones

  1. Connect both phones to the same Wi-Fi network.
  2. Android: open Receive and tap Start receiving. A QR code, the phone's IP address and a 6-digit PIN appear.
  3. iPhone: open Send. iOS asks to find devices on your local network; tap Allow.
  4. Tap Scan QR, allow camera access, and point the camera at the Android screen. The receiver is now saved under "Receivers".

Can't scan? Tap the receiver marked Found on your Wi-Fi · tap to pair and type the PIN, or use Enter IP with the address and PIN shown on Android.

Don't skip "Allow"

If you tap Don't Allow on the local network prompt, the iPhone can't reach the receiver, and you won't see an error that explains why. You can turn it back on under Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network → Lucero Sync.

Your first sync

  1. On the iPhone, select the receiver and tap Sync now.
  2. When iOS asks about photo access, choose Allow Full Access. With "Limited Access", only the photos you pick get backed up.
  3. Lucero scans your library, works out what's new, and starts sending. You'll see the current file, its progress, and counts of files sent, skipped and failed.
  4. Keep both apps open and both phones on chargers. The app keeps the screen awake by itself. A large library can take a few hours the first time.
Interrupted? That's fine

If the Wi-Fi drops, a phone locks or you tap Stop, nothing is lost. Tap Sync now again and it continues from where it stopped, even partway through a video.

If you use iCloud's Optimize iPhone Storage, the iPhone downloads full-quality originals from iCloud as it goes, so it needs internet access too.

Everyday use

  1. Android: Receive → Start receiving.
  2. iPhone: Send → Sync now.

Only photos taken since the last sync are sent. Photos already on the receiver are never copied twice, even if you reinstall the app or pair again.

Where photos are saved

On Android, backups are organised by the date each photo was taken:

Pictures/LuceroSync/
  2026/
    09/
      IMG_4021.HEIC
      IMG_4021.MOV      ← Live Photo motion clip
      IMG_4022.JPG
      IMG_4023.MOV      ← video
    10/
      …
TypeWhat you get
PhotosThe original file (HEIC, JPEG, RAW/ProRAW), unedited, with location and camera data intact.
VideosThe original MOV/MP4, including slo-mo and cinematic recordings.
Live PhotosTwo files with the same name: the still and its MOV clip.
Edited photosThe original, before edits. Edits made after a photo is synced aren't re-sent.

Android may not show HEIC files in older gallery apps. Google Photos and recent Samsung and Pixel galleries open them fine.

Other directions

Every phone can both send and receive. To back up an Android phone to an iPhone, start Receive on the iPhone and Send from Android. On iPhone, received files appear in the Files app under On My iPhone → Lucero Sync. You can also pair one sender with several receivers; each keeps its own sync history.

iPhone as receiver

Files received on an iPhone live inside the app, and iOS deletes them if you uninstall Lucero Sync. Copy them out with the Files app first. Android receivers save to Pictures/LuceroSync, which survives uninstalling.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
The iPhone app closes immediately after openingBuild the latest version. Old builds lack the UIScene setup that recent iOS versions require.
"Waiting for receiver…" foreverMake sure Android shows Receive with the server started, both phones are on the same network, and local network access is allowed on iPhone. Guest Wi-Fi and "AP/client isolation" block the connection.
The receiver never shows up as "Found on your Wi-Fi"Some routers block Bonjour/mDNS. Use Scan QR or Enter IP instead; that works fine.
"Receiver rejected the PIN"Delete the receiver on iPhone (trash icon) and scan the QR code again.
Some items failedUsually iCloud originals that couldn't be downloaded. Connect to the internet and tap Retry failed.
Photos aren't in the Android GalleryAll files access wasn't granted, so files went to the app's private folder. Grant it in the Receive tab and sync again.
The app won't open after a week (iPhone)The free signing expired. Reinstall it with flutter run --release, or refresh it in AltStore or SideStore.

Still stuck? Open an issue and include the lines from the Receive screen's log.

Reinstalling & resetting

  • Reinstalling the sender erases its sync history, so the next sync rescans everything. The receiver still recognises files it already has and skips them, so nothing is duplicated; it just takes longer.
  • Deleting a backed-up file on the receiver makes the next sync restore it. Backups are meant to be complete.
  • Deleting photos on the iPhone never deletes them from the backup.
