01 — Why

Your phones are in the same room. Your photos shouldn't take a detour.

The usual way

Cloud backup

iPhone→ internet→ someone's server→ internet→ Android
  • Monthly storage subscription
  • Every photo leaves your home
  • Limited by your upload speed
  • Often re-compressed along the way
The Lucero way

Straight across your Wi-Fi

iPhone→ your router→ Android
  • Free, with nothing to sign up for
  • Nothing leaves your network
  • As fast as your local Wi-Fi
  • Bit-for-bit originals, verified by SHA-256
02 — Contact sheet

Small app. Careful with every frame.

FR 01A

Originals, untouched

HEIC, RAW and ProRAW photos and full-quality video arrive exactly as they were taken, with EXIF intact and no re-compression.

FR 02A

Live Photos, in pairs

The still and its motion clip arrive together as IMG_0001.HEIC + IMG_0001.MOV. A Live Photo only counts as synced once both halves are safe.

FR 03A

Never a duplicate

The sender keeps a ledger of what's been synced. The receiver double-checks every file by content hash and metadata before accepting it.

FR 04A

Picks up mid-file

If the Wi-Fi drops or the app closes, the next sync continues from the exact byte it reached, even halfway through a 4 GB video.

FR 05A

Pair in one scan

The receiver shows a QR code. Scan it once and the phones remember each other. Bonjour discovery finds the receiver again if its IP address changes.

FR 06A

Any direction

iPhone to Android is the main use, but either phone can send or receive. You can even back one phone up to several others.

03 — Develop

Three steps, then just tap Sync.

Start the receiver

On Android, open the Receive tab and tap Start. It shows a QR code and a 6-digit PIN.

Pair your iPhone

On iPhone, open Send and scan the code. The phones remember each other from then on.

Sync now

Lucero compares your library with what's already been backed up and sends only what's new, every time.

05 — Questions

Good to know.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

There's no catch. It's an MIT-licensed open-source project with no servers to pay for, so there's nothing to charge for. If you'd like to give something back, star the repo or contribute.

Why isn't it on the App Store or Play Store?

Play Store policy restricts the "all files access" permission that lets backups land in your Gallery, and App Store distribution needs a paid developer account. For now, Android installs from a downloadable APK, and iPhone installs by building the app with a free Apple ID or sideloading it with AltStore or SideStore. The guide walks through both.

Do both phones need to stay open during a sync?

Yes, for now. iOS doesn't let apps run long uploads in the background, so keep Lucero open and the phone plugged in. The app keeps the screen awake for you. Later syncs only send new photos, so they're quick.

What if I use iCloud "Optimize iPhone Storage"?

That works. Lucero asks iOS for the full-quality original, and iOS downloads it from iCloud first if needed, so the iPhone needs an internet connection for those photos.

Is the transfer encrypted?

Not yet. Transfers use plain HTTP on your local network, and the receiver only accepts devices that know its PIN. It's built for your home Wi-Fi; avoid using it on public networks. Encrypted transfers are on the roadmap. See the security policy.

Will deleting a photo on Android make it re-sync?

Yes, by design. If a backed-up file is missing from the receiver's folder, the next sync restores it. Photos you delete on the iPhone stay in the backup.
